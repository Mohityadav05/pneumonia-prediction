COMMON MEDICATIONS PRESCRIBED FOR PNEUMONIA
(Educational reference only — dosing must always come from the prescribing doctor)

ANTIBIOTICS (for bacterial pneumonia)
- Amoxicillin: A common first-line antibiotic for mild bacterial pneumonia.
  Typically taken with food to reduce stomach upset. Common side effects include
  nausea, diarrhea, and rash.
- Azithromycin: Often prescribed as a short course (e.g., "Z-pack"). Used for
  atypical bacterial pneumonia. Can affect heart rhythm in rare cases, so doctors
  usually check for existing heart conditions first.
- Levofloxacin / Moxifloxacin (fluoroquinolones): Used for more resistant
  infections. Can cause tendon issues in rare cases and interacts with dairy,
  antacids, and some supplements — timing matters.
- Doxycycline: Another common option; can cause sun sensitivity, so sunscreen
  and protective clothing are advised while taking it.

ANTIVIRALS (for viral pneumonia, e.g., influenza-related)
- Oseltamivir (Tamiflu): Most effective when started within 48 hours of symptom
  onset. Common side effects include nausea and headache.

SUPPORTIVE MEDICATIONS
- Antipyretics (paracetamol/acetaminophen or ibuprofen): Used to manage fever and
  discomfort. Should not exceed labeled maximum daily doses.
- Cough suppressants or expectorants: Sometimes prescribed, though productive
  coughing helps clear the lungs, so doctors may advise against fully suppressing it.
- Bronchodilators (e.g., albuterol inhalers): May be prescribed if wheezing or
  underlying asthma/COPD is present.

GENERAL MEDICATION PRECAUTIONS
- Always finish the prescribed antibiotic course completely.
- Take medications at the prescribed times/spacing — some antibiotics require
  even spacing throughout the day for effectiveness.
- Report any rash, swelling, difficulty breathing, or severe GI symptoms to a
  doctor immediately — these can indicate an allergic reaction.
- Never combine medications or adjust dosages without consulting the prescribing
  doctor or pharmacist, especially with existing conditions or other medications.
